# Flavor And Budget / 好吃与性价比

Use this reference when designing dishes, correcting flavor, choosing ingredients, or optimizing grocery cost.

## Evidence Base / 依据

Primary budget sources:

- MyPlate Shop Smart unit price guidance: https://www.myplate.gov/eathealthy/budget/budget-price-tag
- USDA SNAP-Ed budget cooking resources: https://snaped.fns.usda.gov/resources/nutrition-education-materials/cooking

## Flavor Architecture / 好吃结构

Build every savory meal with:

- Base / 底味: salt, soy sauce, bouillon, miso, cheese, fish sauce, oyster sauce, or fermented bean paste.
- Aroma / 香味: garlic, ginger, scallion, onion, chili, cumin, curry powder, five-spice, black pepper, sesame oil.
- Texture / 口感: browned protein, crisp vegetables, soft starch, crunchy topping, or juicy sauce.
- Freshness / 清爽: vinegar, citrus, tomato, pickles, cucumber, herbs, scallion, cilantro.
- Fat / 油脂: cooking oil, chili oil, butter, tahini, peanut butter, cheese, or rendered meat fat.

## Correction Loop / 试味纠偏

Taste near the end, then fix one axis at a time:

| Problem / 问题 | Fix / 修正 |
|---|---|
| Bland / 寡淡 | Add salt/soy/bouillon in tiny increments |
| Flat / 闷 | Add vinegar, lemon, tomato, pickle juice, or herbs |
| Harsh salty / 死咸 | Add starch, water, unsalted veg, sugar, or acid |
| Too oily / 腻 | Add acid, fresh veg, herbs, or serve with rice |
| Too spicy / 太辣 | Add dairy, peanut/tahini, sugar, starch, or more main ingredients |
| Watery / 水 | Reduce uncovered, thicken with cornstarch slurry, or drain veg before saucing |

## Reliable Ratios / 稳定比例

- Stir-fry sauce for 2 servings: 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp oyster sauce or hoisin + 1 tsp vinegar + 1 tsp sugar + 1 tsp cornstarch + 3 tbsp water.
- Rice bowl sauce for 2 servings: 2 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp vinegar/citrus + 1 tbsp chili oil/sesame/peanut + 1 tsp sugar.
- Tomato pasta base for 2 servings: 1 tbsp oil + 2 cloves garlic + 1 tbsp tomato paste + 1 cup canned tomato + salt + chili/oregano.
- Curry bowl for 2 servings: 1 tbsp oil + aromatics + 1.5 tbsp curry paste/powder + 1 cup coconut milk or broth + protein + vegetables.
- Soup noodle bowl for 1 serving: 2 cups broth/water + bouillon/soy + aromatics + 80-100 g dry noodles + veg + egg/tofu/meat.

## Cost-Per-Serving Method / 每份成本法

1. Convert shelf price to unit price: dollars per pound, ounce, gram, or serving.
2. Compare edible yield: bone-in meat, peels, and spoiled produce reduce value.
3. Prefer overlap: 3 dishes sharing scallions, cabbage, eggs, rice, tofu, or ground meat usually beat 3 unrelated recipes.
4. Buy shelf-stable flavor, fresh produce modestly, frozen vegetables generously.
5. Freeze expensive proteins in meal-size portions.
6. Choose one "anchor protein" and one "anchor vegetable" per shopping trip.

## Budget Ingredient Ladder / 预算食材梯队

Low-cost anchors:

- Protein: eggs, tofu, lentils, beans, canned tuna, chicken thighs, ground turkey/pork/beef on sale, peanut butter.
- Starch: rice, pasta, potatoes, oats, tortillas, noodles.
- Vegetables: cabbage, carrots, onions, frozen broccoli, frozen peas/corn, napa cabbage, cucumbers, canned tomatoes.
- Flavor: soy sauce, vinegar, garlic, ginger, chili crisp/oil, curry powder, bouillon, sesame oil, tomato paste.

Splurge only when it transforms multiple meals: good chili oil, oyster sauce, parmesan, kimchi, miso, fish sauce, frozen dumplings.

## Store Tactics / 买菜策略

- Asian market: rice, noodles, tofu, sauces, leafy greens, mushrooms, scallions.
- Aldi/Walmart: eggs, dairy, frozen veg, canned tomatoes/beans, chicken, pasta, tortillas.
- Costco/Sam's: only if freezer space exists; portion immediately.
- Sale proteins: buy only if there is a same-day cooking/freezing plan.
- Unit price tags can use different units; normalize before comparing.

## Deliciousness Checklist / 美味检查

Before presenting a plan, verify:

- The dish has salt/umami, aroma, fat, acid/freshness, and texture.
- There is a finishing step after cooking: herbs, scallion, vinegar, chili oil, sesame, cheese, or crunch.
- Vegetables are not all overcooked; at least one element stays fresh or crisp.
- Sauces are thick enough to cling to rice/noodles unless the dish is a soup.
- The user has a low-effort rescue: egg, rice, frozen veg, soy sauce, vinegar, chili oil.
